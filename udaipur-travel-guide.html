City of Lakes

Udaipur Travel Guide

Everything you need to plan a trip to Udaipur — when to go, what to see, how to get there, and where to stay once you land.

Udaipur earns its nickname — the "City of Lakes" — honestly. Lake Pichola sits at the centre of almost everything worth seeing here: the City Palace rises straight out of its western bank, Jag Mandir floats on an island in the middle of it, and most of the old city's havelis and ghats face the water. It's compact enough to see the highlights in two focused days, and calm enough — compared to Jaipur or Jodhpur — that three or four days here doesn't feel like too many.

This guide covers the practical stuff first: when to come, how long to stay, how to actually get here. Then the places worth your time, and where groups and families tend to stay when the old city itself gets too crowded for comfort.

When to Visit

Oct – Mar · Best

Pleasant 15–28°C days, clear skies, comfortable for walking the old city and boat rides on Lake Pichola. Peak season — book stays early around Diwali and New Year.

Apr – Jun · Hot

Temperatures climb to 38–42°C by afternoon. Mornings and evenings are still workable if you plan sightseeing around the heat — and hotel/villa prices tend to be lower.

Jul – Sep · Monsoon

The lakes fill up and the surrounding Aravalli hills turn green — genuinely the most photogenic time to visit, with occasional heavy rain days that can disrupt outdoor plans.

Top Places to Visit

Most of these sit within a short walk or auto ride of each other around Lake Pichola — you don't need a full day for any single one.

01

City Palace

The largest palace complex in Rajasthan, built over nearly 400 years. Courtyards, mirror-work halls, and views straight down onto Lake Pichola from the upper terraces.

02

Lake Pichola

Take an evening boat ride for the best view of the City Palace and the Lake Palace lit up against the water — the classic Udaipur photo.

03

Jag Mandir

A marble island palace reachable only by boat from the City Palace jetty — quieter than the mainland sights, good for an unhurried hour.

04

Saheliyon ki Bari

A garden of fountains built for royal attendants, with a lotus pool and marble elephants — a relaxed stop, especially good early morning.

05

Fateh Sagar Lake

Less crowded than Pichola, with a cable car up Karni Mata temple hill for a full city view and sunset over the water.

06

Bagore ki Haveli

A restored 18th-century haveli on the Gangaur Ghat waterfront — worth timing your visit for the evening Rajasthani folk dance show.

07

Monsoon Palace

A hilltop palace above the city, best reached just before sunset for panoramic views over Udaipur and the surrounding lakes.

08

Ambrai Ghat

The quietest, most local spot for a lakeside evening — locals, chai stalls, and arguably the best unobstructed view of the City Palace at dusk.

How to Reach Udaipur

By Air: Maharana Pratap Airport (UDR) has direct flights from Delhi, Mumbai, Jaipur, Bangalore and Ahmedabad. It's about 25 minutes from the city centre by cab.

By Train: Udaipur City railway station connects to Delhi, Mumbai, Jaipur, Ahmedabad and Chittorgarh, with the Mewar Express and several overnight trains being the most common routes for tourists.

By Road: Udaipur sits on NH8, roughly 660 km from Delhi, 260 km from Ahmedabad, and 400 km from Jaipur — a comfortable overnight or early-morning drive from any of the three, and a common stop on Rajasthan road trips.

Getting Around Once You're There

The old city around Lake Pichola is genuinely walkable — most of the top sights sit within 15–20 minutes of each other on foot. For anything further out (Fateh Sagar, Monsoon Palace, or a villa outside the city centre), autos and app-based cabs are easy to find and inexpensive. Self-drive scooty and bike rentals are widely available near the old city for a day or two of independent exploring, and most hotels or villas can point you to a reliable local operator.

Where to Stay

The old city near Lake Pichola puts you closest to the main sights, but it gets noisy and cramped for groups — narrow lanes, small rooms, little privacy. For families, bachelor parties, or any group of six or more, a private villa outside the immediate old-city crush tends to work better: your own pool, your own kitchen, and 15–30 minutes to the main sights by auto rather than a five-minute walk through crowds.
